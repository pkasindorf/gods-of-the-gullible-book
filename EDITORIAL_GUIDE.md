## Source philosophy

The `brain` folder is a living archive, not a contract. It contains notes gathered over more than a year. Some are polished fragments; some are single phrases; some are duplicate ideas; some are jokes, links, prompts, or placeholders. Every note is a potential ingredient, not an instruction.

When developing the manuscript:

- Preserve my underlying question before polishing the wording.
- Treat competing notes as possibilities to reconcile, not contradictions to erase.
- Separate autobiographical experience, personal theory, research, and rhetorical provocation.
- Do not silently turn an interesting note into a factual assertion or a required chapter.
- Keep a visible record of unresolved choices.

## Authorial stance

The introduction should make the following promises, drawn from the archive:

- I am not trying to convert the reader.
- I am not trying to belittle or destroy a belief that supports someone's good moral life.
- I am not standing on a claim of perfect certainty.
- I propose an unproven, unscientific personal theory and invites the reader to question it.
- The reader should question my words as seriously as inherited religious and political beliefs.

These promises sit alongside the voice's "evidence-backed" standard without conflict. Claims about the world (history, psychology, scripture, what a group did) need evidence. My own model is labeled openly as personal theory. See [voice.md](voice.md) → Evidence and theory.

## Language and concept guardrails

### Gullible

Use *gullible* deliberately and compassionately. The intended meaning is a human vulnerability to accepting information, authority, emotion, and social consensus—not stupidity, moral failure, or a diagnosis. The phrase “I was born gullible” should open identification rather than humiliation.

### Belief, fact, opinion, and proof

The practical guide can repeatedly ask:

- Is this a fact, fiction, interpretation, or opinion?
- What evidence supports it?
- What would change my mind?
- Who is making the claim, and what might they want?
- Am I defending a claim because it is true, or because it has become part of my identity?

“Require proof” is a strong motto, but the book should define what counts as adequate evidence for different kinds of claims. Avoid implying that every meaningful personal value can be proved like a laboratory measurement.

### Cognitive dissonance

Use cognitive dissonance as both a researched concept and a lived experience. The Santa scene provides a clear first example: at age ten, I knew Santa was not real but performed belief to protect holiday magic and please my parents. The tension was between private knowledge and public behavior, with love, belonging, and tradition making the performance feel worthwhile.

The Hartford Institute's entry summarizes the classic theory as discomfort arising from inconsistencies among a person's cognitions or between cognitions and behavior, followed by efforts to reduce that discomfort. Use the concept carefully: not every contradiction is pathological, and people can sometimes hold tension without immediately resolving it.

Starting reference: [Hartford Institute — Cognitive Dissonance](https://hirr.hartfordinternational.edu/articles/4203/)

### The Inner Council

Present **The Inner Council** as the primary metaphor. It was renamed from "The Inner Court" on 2026-10-15. The concept is unchanged. The Choice Gate, Decision Triangle, Inner Tiebreaker, and Gullibility Filter can appear later as alternate lenses or chapter-level names.

The Inner Council is a three-function model. It is a personal metaphor informed by neuroscience, not a literal claim about brain anatomy.

**Current working map** (correct so far, open for further discussion and elaboration):

- **Force 1 — biology.** Instinct, evolutionary drive, survival fear, desire. "Biology" is only a summary label. The book will go much deeper here.
- **Force 2 — institution.** Culture, dogma, parental rules, community norms, inherited belief. Force 2 holds beliefs the Thirdspace has already deliberated, committed, and approved, many of them inherited. That stored material biases future decisions toward culture, dogma, and rule-based notions of right and wrong.
- **Force 3 — the judge.** Force 3 is the Thirdspace. The highest level of thought, above Forces 1 and 2. This is where critical thinking happens. It is the judge of the council and always makes the final decision. Force 3 is also:
  - where truth claims are committed to the mind as fact
  - where moral decisions are made
  - where God is found
  - the ultimate target of exploitation

Together, the three form the trilectical Inner Council, with Force 3 above all.

**How the Thirdspace develops.** The Thirdspace is always there. It is never missing and is never "unbuilt." Like a child, it can be developed or underdeveloped, mature or immature. An underdeveloped Thirdspace still makes the final decision, and it makes a poor one.

**How Forces 1 and 2 relate.** They usually fight, though not always. Together they represent the binary final choice. Even when choosing among 31 ice-cream flavors, it always comes down to the last two, perhaps chocolate or vanilla. Turn left or turn right. Should I start the conversation, or should I keep my mouth shut? When a fight occurs, the final decision is always Force 3's.

**What "binary" means.** Binary describes the final choice, not the world. Life offers many options, but every decision narrows to the last two before Force 3 rules.

**Where gullibility lives: the whole council, signed off by Force 3.** Gullibility is when the Inner Council accepts claims without demanding evidence. Firstspace and Secondspace deliberate, and the Thirdspace signs off without asking for proof. Force 3 is where we commit truth claims to the brain as facts. Corruption occurs when those facts are unsubstantiated, and it affects the council's overall decisions. Gullibility only becomes visible when we realize that certain facts are not real, such as Santa Claus or the claim that the election was stolen. The corruption exists from the moment an unsubstantiated fact is committed, but it is invisible until hindsight. A person can be gullible right now without knowing it.

**What corrupts the council.** Only external forces can corrupt the council. Force 1 is biology, a member of the council, so it cannot be a source of corruption. Self-serving external forces get claims approved without proof. Once approved, a claim lives in Force 2 and biases future decisions.

**Cognitive dissonance in the council.** As the Thirdspace develops and matures, it may conflict with truths held in Force 2. That conflict is cognitive dissonance.

### God, spirituality, and prayer

The book can distinguish:

- God as an external supernatural claim
- God as an internal metaphor or conversation
- God as a name for conscience, wisdom, or the third function of the Inner Council
- Religious practice as a source of ritual, community, grief processing, meaning, or behavior change

Do not make an atheist reader give up meaningful ritual, love, awe, or spirituality. Do not make a religious reader accept my interpretation in order to benefit from the exercises.

### Spiritual bypassing

Treat spiritual bypassing as a recurring diagnostic question, not as an accusation against spirituality itself. The working definition is the use of spiritual ideas or practices to sidestep unresolved emotional issues, psychological wounds, conflict, responsibility, or difficult action. Examples may include using “everything happens for a reason,” “good vibes only,” “thoughts and prayers,” or “it is God's plan” to close down an honest conversation or dismiss another person's pain.

Keep the distinction clear: prayer, meditation, ritual, forgiveness, acceptance, and positive thinking may be healthy sources of comfort, meaning, and resilience. They become bypassing when they function as a shield against reality, accountability, grief, anger, boundaries, or action. The editorial test is: **Does this practice help the person face the problem, or help the person avoid it?**

Starting reference: [Verywell Mind — Spiritual Bypassing as a Defense Mechanism](https://www.verywellmind.com/what-is-spiritual-bypassing-5081640)

## Fact and research guardrails

The prefrontal cortex is associated with executive functions including decision-making, self-control, planning, working memory, and adapting behavior. That supports using neuroscience as context for the Inner Council, but it does not establish a literal three-section brain or a simple left/right binary system.

- Cleveland Clinic, “Prefrontal Cortex”: <https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/prefrontal-cortex>

## Potential practical tools

- The Proof Test: What is the claim, and what evidence supports it?
- The Fact/Opinion/Fiction Label: What kind of statement is this?
- The Agenda Check: Who benefits if I accept this?
- The Identity Check: What part of me feels threatened by questioning this?
- The Fear and Hate Check: Is urgency or an enemy being used to force unity?
- The Other-Side Practice: Can I state the strongest version of the opposing view?
- The Inner Council Pause: What are the choices, what are the consequences, and what does my considered judgment decide?
- The “I Don't Know” Practice: Can uncertainty be admitted without immediately filling it with a story?
- The Spiritual Bypass Check: Is this spiritual language helping me face the problem, or helping me avoid it?

If a charged term or quotation is retained, it must serve a clear analytical purpose and not become casual provocation. The writer's lived experience should anchor the discussion rather than broad labels applied to strangers.
